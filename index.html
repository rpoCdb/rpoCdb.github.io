Why rpoC?

rpoC encodes the β' subunit of RNA polymerase - an essential, single-copy, protein-coding gene present across all bacteria.

Universal & single-copy

A conserved priming region enables reliable amplification across diverse bacterial lineages, and one copy per genome avoids the abundance bias of multi-copy markers like 16S rRNA.

High phylogenetic resolution

Discriminates taxa at the species and strain level, surpassing the limitations of ribosomal RNA genes.

Resolves the toughest complexes

Separates taxa that 16S rRNA cannot, including M. tuberculosis / M. africanum and the B. cereus / anthracis / thuringiensis complex.

Standard workflow, purpose-built tools

A routine PCR and Illumina sequencing protocol, backed by a dedicated reference database (rpoCdb) and a dedicated analysis pipeline (CUSTARD).

What this primer may miss

Four phyla account for 76% of the database. Coverage past that is uneven — worth knowing before you rely on it for an unusual sample type.

Pseudomonadota, 27% Bacillota, 21% Bacteroidota, 14% Actinomycetota, 14% All other phyla, 24%
Reduced coverage

The current V521 primer's degeneracy has some coverage limitations for phyla such as Planctomycetota, Armatimonadota, and Elusimicrobiota.

Complete gaps

It completely misses small and uncultured candidate lineages such as Joyebacterota, UBA4055, and JACQOV01 — likely because the corresponding metagenome-assembled genomes lack a detectable rpoC gene (possibly from incomplete assemblies), or because the primer needs further optimization for these phyla.
